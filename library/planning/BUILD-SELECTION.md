## Why 101 is the right count

The number is an outcome. It was not chosen first.

101 is what remained after capping every over-represented register the critique identified,
protecting the high-range familiars that a scored method under-rewards, and keeping enough ambition
that the laboratory is worth running. The reserve is nearly as large as the selection — 106
concepts — which is the honest signal that this is a _selection_ rather than a rubber stamp.

It is above a hundred and that is not a failure of triage. A section library's value is coverage, and
the shape of the selected distribution is the argument: the four families a service business actually
buys against — proof (10), comparison (8), process and timeline (8) and dense
information (8) — together carry 34 of the 101, alongside 10 editorial concepts. Those are
precisely the families the research found near-absent from every catalogue surveyed, so they are the
part of the set that cannot simply be downloaded and the part that has to carry several genuinely
different answers each.

The registers that repeat easily are held down instead: rails at 3, stacked cards at 4,
interactive relationship at 3. No category was padded to a quota, and the counts differ by more
than three-to-one across the set, which is what a distribution shaped by usefulness rather than by
symmetry looks like.

One rejection only. Everything else that did not make the set is **reserve, not refuse** — most were
demoted for register saturation rather than weakness, which is a statement about the set, not about
the concept.

## Familiar and ambitious

The critique's sharpest finding was that **difficulty had been rated purely as engineering cost** —
every high-difficulty entry moved, responded or held scroll, and not one static composition was rated
above medium. Ambition was being equated with mechanism.

The selection corrects for that rather than inheriting it. Genuinely hard _static_ compositions —
a broadsheet column shift with optical alignment, a sans-and-monospace receipt grid on a shared
baseline, a full specification sheet, a sentence cut deliberately across a fold — are selected as
first-class work, not as filler because they were cheap to rate.

**Protected for range, not for score.** These carry the library and would lose a scoring contest
against anything dramatic, so they are recorded here as selected on range: `SEC-020` alternating
artefact rows, `SEC-057` unequal diptych, `SEC-031` consequence-footed claim cards, `SEC-162`
deliverables ledger, `SEC-160` specification sheet, `SEC-182` breakout ladder. `SEC-182` is the
highest-leverage entry in the whole set because it is a width vocabulary other sections inherit
rather than a section in itself.

**Ambition kept.** `SEC-107` held interface, `SEC-114` grid zoom-through, `SEC-106` shortlist tray
stack, `SEC-105` stack ending as an index, `SEC-100` cards leaving a line, `SEC-011` offer specimen
rig, `SEC-216` true-duration track. These are structural ambition — memorable and still able to
accept different content — rather than effects that can only be themselves.

**One-off artwork capped.** The critique counted about ten concepts that can only ever be themselves.
The selection keeps a small number and demotes the rest, so the set stays a library rather than a
showreel.

## First build wave (19)

Balanced, not front-loaded with the hardest work — the point of a wave is to learn what building
actually costs across the ordinary case, not only the extreme one.

**Familiar low-risk baselines** — establish the house style and prove the foundation under real
sections: `SEC-001`, `SEC-020`, `SEC-057`, `SEC-162`, `SEC-182`.

**Under-served service jobs, still low risk** — the families that justify the library existing:
`SEC-067`, `SEC-070`, `SEC-119`, `SEC-145`, `SEC-147`.

**Medium interactive** — exercises disclosure, state, filtering, sticky behaviour and annotation:
`SEC-082`, `SEC-133`, `SEC-163`, `SEC-098`, `SEC-144`.

**Motion mechanism, one of each kind** — `SEC-198` (CSS view timelines, no JavaScript) and
`SEC-193` (GSAP with whole-line masking).

**The uncertain ones** — deliberately few, because these are where the cost estimate is least
trustworthy: `SEC-107` (what a pin really costs in a React lifecycle) and `SEC-115` (whether a
bounded hold reads as intentional).

`SEC-182` should be built first regardless of order: it is the width vocabulary the others inherit.

## Later sequencing

1. **Establish shared mechanism before spreading it.** One pin/scrub hook, one reduced-motion branch,
   one accordion contract, one scroll-container keyboard contract — built once in wave one and reused.
   The research is explicit that these are the same code every time and that reproducing them per
   section is the largest defect source.
2. **Build a register's baseline before its ambitious member.** `SEC-098` before `SEC-105`;
   `SEC-067` before `SEC-069`.
3. **Alternate cost.** Never two build-high sections consecutively — the wave reviews are worth more
   when they arrive at regular intervals.
4. **Hold the scroll-capturing five apart.** Building them in sequence would tune them against each
   other rather than against ordinary sections.
5. **Promote from reserve only through the coverage test** — what job or spatial behaviour is now
   missing — never because a reserve concept looked good in isolation.

## Risks to revisit after the first wave

- **Is 101 still right?** The feasibility scores are estimates. If wave one runs materially
  over, the honest response is to move concepts to reserve, not to build them worse.
- **Does the reduced-motion obligation cost what we think?** Roughly half the set needs a separately
  designed static composition rather than disabled tweens. That is build cost, and it has never been
  measured here.
- **Do the scroll-capturing five read as five, or as too many?** Judge them composed onto one page,
  which is the condition under which the research says they fail.
- **Is the static work actually hard enough?** The critique's charge was that ambition had been
  equated with mechanism. If the selected static compositions turn out cheap and unremarkable to
  build, the correction did not take and the compositional bar needs raising.
- **Does the catalogue need the taxonomy before more sections land?** The registry's `tags` field is
  still a placeholder. Around twenty entries is where an untagged index stops being browsable.
