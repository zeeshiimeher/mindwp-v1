# Section taxonomy

The category system, identifier scheme, tag vocabulary and concept-versus-variant rule for the
library longlist in [COMPONENT-LONGLIST.md](./COMPONENT-LONGLIST.md).

Built from the research in [RESEARCH.md](./RESEARCH.md), before the longlist was written.

Library-local planning, not a website authority.

## What shaped these categories

Two findings from the research decided the shape.

**Existing catalogues are severely lopsided.** shadcnblocks ships 311 Feature blocks and 247 Hero
blocks against 4 Process, 10 Compare and 3 Case Study. Tailwind Plus ships 15 Feature Sections
against 3 Bento Grids and no comparison family at all. Every open collection surveyed converges on
the same seven families — hero, logo cloud, features, stats, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, CTA. A page
assembled from any of them has the same skeleton as every competitor's.

**The under-served families are exactly the ones a service business needs.** Comparison, process,
proof and artefact, case-study excerpt, state change, dense information and editorial composition are
where a buyer's real questions get answered, and they are close to absent from the catalogues.

So the allocation deliberately over-weights those families and keeps the saturated ones tight. This
is not a judgement that feature grids are inferior — a feature grid with high styling range earns its
place. It is a judgement about where building adds something that cannot simply be downloaded.

### Category definitions

**A — Editorial and typography-led.** The composition is type, measure, space and rule. Includes
openers, chapter dividers, pull-quote and breakout ladders, sidenote and margin structures, manifesto
statements, multi-column prose, type specimens, annotation layers. Excludes anything whose subject is
an image or a diagram.

**B — Feature and capability explainers.** Sections whose job is "here is what this does" for a small
set of related things. Includes icon-led rows, alternating feature pairs, feature-with-media splits,
capability ledgers. The bar for entry is high because the family is saturated.

**C — Card systems and grids.** Repeating units on a regular grid: three-column, 3×3, four-up,
filterable grids, card-to-detail expansion, batch-revealed grids. Regularity is the defining property
— unequal weighting belongs in D.

**D — Bento, asymmetric and masonry.** Compositions where cell size or position encodes importance,
or where the grid is deliberately irregular. Includes bento, offset grids, ragged masonry walls,
scroll-reflowed grids.

**E — Split and side-by-side.** Two regions in deliberate relation across one section: diptychs,
sticky-column-plus-scrolling-rail, two-speed splits, draggable splitters, unequal ratios.

**F — Comparison and decision support.** Sections that help a reader choose: matrices, versus layouts,
difference-only comparators, structural recommendations, cost-of-inaction ledgers, objection matrices.

**G — Tabs, accordions and disclosure.** Content the reader reveals: tab sets, accordions, FAQ
structures, disclosure ladders, semantic zoom, in-place glossaries.

**H — Sliders, carousels and rails.** Lateral movement the reader drives: shelves, drag rails with
inertia, native scroll-snap carousels, expand-to-overlay rails. Autoplay is not a concept here.

**I — Stacked, overlapping and sticky cards.** Cards related by depth, overlap or sequence: sticky
card decks, fanned stacks, offset pairs, collapsing stacks, header-only stacks.

**J — Pinned, horizontal and multi-part scroll.** Sections that hold scroll in place or redirect it
sideways, and continuous multi-stage experiences catalogued as one concept. Capped at 12 deliberately
— the research is unambiguous that these do not survive repetition on one page.

**K — Process, sequence and timeline.** Ordered material: numbered processes, drawn spines,
dependency chains, branching sequences, timelines by era, duration, trajectory or two-track.

**L — State change and before/after.** One thing shown in two or more states: draggable comparisons,
range-driven wipes, peel-away pairs, in-place diffs, toggles that retime a whole section, typographic
redlines.

**M — Proof, artefact and case study.** Evidence: real deliverables, annotated artefacts, redacted
documents, evidence-footed figures, case-study excerpts, bound testimonials, recognition tallies,
client fields.

**N — Dense information and indexes.** Density as the statement: specification sheets, expandable
tables, deliverables ledgers, alphabetical indexes with live filtering, status matrices, coverage
grids.

**O — Media, image and video.** Media as subject: poster-first video bands, transcript-paired video,
un-cropping clips, image galleries, sticky-caption figures, re-cropping sequences.

**P — Breakout, full-bleed and layered.** Width and depth as devices: breakout ladders on named grid
lines, single-edge bleeds, full-bleed statement bands, bounded depth frames, converging planes.

**Q — Motion-led composition.** Motion inside normal scroll — the section never captures it: batch
reveals, masked line reveals, marquees and tickers, velocity-reactive rails, clip-path wipes,
scroll-drawn SVG, CSS view-timeline ladders.

**R — Interactive relationship.** Sections whose subject is a relationship between things: node maps,
cross-column dimming matrices, coupling diagrams, annotated hotspot artefacts, drag-to-explore fields.

## Overlap rules

Most concepts touch several categories. The rules resolve that deterministically:

1. **Primary category is the one that owns the concept's reason for existing.** A comparison rendered
   as a dense table is F if the point is choosing, N if the point is density.
2. **Spatial behaviour outranks subject when the behaviour is the concept.** A process shown on a
   pinned horizontal track is J, not K, because remove the pin and it is a different section. A
   process on a drawn spine that scrolls normally is K, because the spine survives.
3. **J and Q are separated by whether the section takes scroll.** If it pins, snaps, or moves content
   sideways under vertical scroll, it is J. If motion happens while the page scrolls normally, it is
   Q.
4. **C and D are separated by whether unequal weighting carries meaning.** Regular grid, C. Meaningful
   irregularity, D.
5. **A concept appears exactly once.** Secondary categories are recorded as cross-references in the
   entry, never as a second ID.
6. **Category is metadata, not identity.** Moving a concept between categories does not change its
   ID.

## Concept versus variant

**A new concept requires a meaningful change in at least one of:** composition; spatial behaviour;
hierarchy; reader activity; interaction; motion logic; the relationship between the content;
responsive transformation; implementation model.

**A variant is a change in:** surface or palette; radius, border, shadow or elevation; typeface or
type treatment; imagery, icon or media choice; alignment; item count; indicator styling; density
within an unchanged structure.

**The test:** _what does the reader do differently?_ If the answer is nothing — they read the same
things in the same order with the same activity — it is a variant, however different it looks.

Worked examples, following the direction given:

- A light card grid and the same grid on navy — **one concept**, two surface variants.
- A static three-card grid and a sticky sequence where each card replaces the last — **two concepts**;
  spatial behaviour and reading experience both change.
- Two sliders differing in colour — **one concept**.
- A conventional content slider and a full-screen pinned carousel with changing background scenes —
  **two concepts**.
- Tailwind Plus's "Offset 2×2 grid" and "Centered 2×2 grid" — **one concept**, two arrangement
  variants. The catalogue names them separately; by this rule they are not separate.

**Variants are recorded inside the parent entry.** They are the preferred way to capture range —
a concept carrying five named variants is more useful than five entries that differ cosmetically.
Most entries carry two to six; the number reflects genuine range, not a quota.

## Static and interactive versions of the same idea

If adding interaction changes only the timing of what appears, it is a **variant**: a card grid and
the same grid with a staggered entrance reveal are one concept.

If adding interaction changes what the reader can do or what they can compare, it is a **separate
concept**: a static comparison table and one where selecting two options renders only that pair are
different, because the second lets the reader ask a question the first cannot answer.

Motion that only decorates never creates a concept.

## Familiar and experimental patterns

Both are first-class. A familiar structure enters on **styling and composition range** — how many
genuinely different sections one concept can produce. An experimental structure enters on
**distinctiveness and what building it teaches**, and is expected to have narrower range.

Neither is ranked above the other, and the longlist is deliberately built to contain both. Ambition
is not a tiebreaker, and neither is safety.

## Identifiers

Permanent IDs of the form `SEC-001` … `SEC-210`, assigned once in category order.

- IDs are **permanent**. They are never reused, never renumbered, and never recycled from a rejected
  concept.
- Category is metadata. A concept that moves from K to J keeps its ID and its position in the file
  may become out of numeric order — that is correct and expected.
- A merged concept keeps its ID in the record marked `MERGE → SEC-nnn`; the surviving concept absorbs
  its idea as a variant.
- Variants are identified within the parent, not with their own IDs.

## Tags for the future catalogue

The suggested tag list has been reorganised into dimensions, and changed in four places where the
research supports something better.

### Mechanism — what the section is built from

`no-motion` · `css-motion` · `scroll-css` · `js-motion` · `gsap-core`

`scroll-css` (view/scroll timelines) is separated from `js-motion` because the research is explicit
that the two express scroll progress differently, cannot be synchronised reliably, and should be
chosen per page rather than per section. `js-motion` means GSAP improves the section; `gsap-core`
means removing GSAP removes the concept.

### Spatial behaviour — the primary discriminator

`static` · `sticky` · `pinned` · `horizontal` · `layered` · `swap-in-place` · `scroll-driven` ·
`user-driven`

This dimension does most of the work of separating concepts from variants, so it is the one tag set
that must be assigned precisely.

### Width

`contained` · `wide` · `breakout` · `full-bleed`

### Density

`density-low` · `density-medium` · `density-high`

### Difficulty

`build-low` · `build-medium` · `build-high` · `experimental`

### Scroll budget — added

`budget-none` · `budget-adds-scroll` · `budget-captures-scroll`

Not in the suggested list, and the single most useful addition. The research is consistent that
pinned and scroll-capturing sections fail by **accumulation** rather than individually — three on one
page is the documented threshold at which readers lose their sense of page length. A tag that makes
the cost visible at compose time prevents that from being discovered late.

### Reduced-motion cost — added

`rm-free` · `rm-designed`

`rm-designed` marks a section that needs a **separately designed static composition** under
`prefers-reduced-motion`, not merely disabled tweens. Reverting a scrub or a pin leaves an empty
stage, and reviewers now explicitly toggle the setting when judging. This is a build-cost tag as much
as an accessibility one.

### Accessibility watch — added

`kbd-path` · `text-integrity` · `motion-sensitive` · `semantics-fragile`

Four specific, documented hazards rather than a vague risk score: `kbd-path` for scroll containers
and drag surfaces that need an explicit non-pointer route; `text-integrity` for split-text work;
`motion-sensitive` for multi-plane parallax and positional depth; `semantics-fragile` for tables that
must not be re-shaped with display properties, and card grids that must remain lists.

### Surface — changed

`surface-critical` only.

Light, dark and mixed-surface were dropped as tags. Under this taxonomy's own rule, surface is a
**variant axis**, so tagging it would contradict the concept-versus-variant rule. `surface-critical`
is kept for the minority of concepts whose composition genuinely depends on a surface change — a
navy panel sitting on paper as the widest value step the system has.

### Not adopted

`GSAP-enhanced` / `GSAP-dependent` were folded into the mechanism dimension. `light` / `dark` /
`mixed surface` were dropped for the reason above. `experimental` was moved into the difficulty
dimension rather than standing alone, since in practice it always describes build risk.
