# MindWP page design

Decide the design. Write no production code.

`docs/DESIGN.md` is the canonical design authority — read and apply it. This skill owns method, not doctrine, and states a rule from elsewhere only where using the method requires it.

## Enter at the stage the material reaches

Work arrives at different stages. Recognise which from what was supplied, not from the words used to ask. State in one line which stage you are entering at, then continue — do not ask the user to classify their own request.

- **Explore a direction** — the page's argument is settled, its experience is not. Produce genuinely different directions, not one direction three ways.
- **Compose a page** — a plan exists; the composition does not. This is the common case and the deliverable below.
- **Specify an approved composition** — the composition is decided or supplied. Develop sections; do not recompose.
- **Critique a plan or direction** — say what is strong, what will fail and why. Do not answer a critique request with a redesign.
- **Redesign an existing page** — see *Redesign and audit-to-rebuild*.
- **Improve one section** — work within the page's existing composition, and check the result against its neighbours.
- **Continue from approved external work** — treat supplied decisions as settled. Identify only real conflicts, unavailable proof, unsupported claims, open commercial terms or serious design and technical problems. Report gaps briefly and continue. Do not reformat a supplied plan into this skill's vocabulary as a precondition.

Do not run every mode. Do not rerun a stage the supplied material already completed.

## The page composition is the primary deliverable

When visual direction is open, decide the page before deciding its sections, and report it for review before developing any section. A composition a person can reject in a few minutes is worth more than a specification nobody reads.

Decide and state:

**Spatial thesis.** One sentence: how this page occupies the screen, and how that changes as the reader moves down it. If the honest answer is "a centred column throughout", say so as a decision and say what makes that right for this material.

**Environments.** How many distinct spatial environments the page has, what each one is, and which matters live inside each. Several matters may share one continuous environment; one matter may need more than one. There is no correct number, and a page whose environment count equals its matter count has not made this decision.

**Width behaviour.** Where the page is contained, wide, full-bleed or deliberately narrow. What leaves the container, and why the meaning needs it to. Width is a decision either way — an unstated envelope is not neutral, it is the container's default deciding on the design's behalf.

**The hero.** Its role, its dominant material, and what it deliberately does not do. Derive it from this page's argument and first visitor decision. Text left with a constructed object right is one construction among many and is not a starting point.

**The largest thing.** The single object or typographic moment that dominates the page, and where it sits. Name it explicitly. If nothing dominates, say why the material does not want a dominant moment.

**Eye path and reading behaviour.** What the eye does from arrival to action, and where reading effort changes — where the reader slows, scans, works through density, or is given room.

**Weight.** Which moments carry the page and which are connective. Focal treatment is a judgement, not a requirement: where meaning, proof or the buyer's decision earns it, say which and why; where none does, a coherent page may be deliberately quieter, and that is a decision to state rather than a gap. Nothing is promoted to make a page look eventful.

**Proof placement.** Where evidence appears relative to the claims it supports, at what scale, and how constructed material is identified.

**Reading paths.** See below.

**Interaction and meaning-bearing motion.** One decision each, at page scale. See below.

**Narrow widths.** What recomposes rather than stacks, and which relationships must survive.

Report this, stop, and let it be reviewed. Build and detailed specification begin after that.

## Reading paths

The strongest single check available before anything is built.

For each moment, name **what the reader actually does** — read one long thing, compare two things, scan labelled items, inspect an artefact, follow a sequence, weigh a choice, operate something. Then count the distinct answers.

A page can carry a grid, a radial diagram, a row of panels, a tab set and an accordion and still ask the reader to do the same thing five times: read a label, read a sentence, repeat. That page is flat, and it will look varied in a specification and identical in a render.

Where more than about half the moments share one reading path, the composition is not finished. This is diagnostic, not a quota — two matters may honestly want the same reading path, and a page whose material is genuinely uniform is allowed to say so.

## Name the material before drawing

Every substantial moment names what it is materially made of. Naming it is the point at which "text in a column" stops being the default answer.

There is no fixed list to choose from, and `DESIGN.md` deliberately keeps none — a taxonomy becomes a menu, and a menu gets worked through. Name what this material actually needs.

Three tests before a payload is accepted:

- **Sketchable in one sentence** without naming layout containers. "A track where four channels converge on one owner" passes. "A two-column split with a card on the right" does not — that is an arrangement, not a material.
- **Legible without its paragraph.** If the object only makes sense once the copy beside it is read, it has not externalised the relationship.
- **An instance, a state or an artefact — not a category.** "A form that arrived at 11:47pm and the person who owns it in the morning" is an instance. "The kinds of enquiry that arrive" is a category, and a category has no shape, so it will be drawn as a labelled box whatever care follows.

The third test catches specifications that pass everything else and still describe a flat page.

Where the material is genuinely quiet, copy and rules alone is a correct answer. When it is the answer for most of a page, say why the page's main meanings do not need stronger expression.

Naming material is not a variety exercise. Two moments may correctly use the same material when their relationships are the same kind, and choosing a weaker payload because a stronger one is "already used" is a decision made for the wrong reason.

## Interaction and meaning-bearing motion

Answer both once, at page scale, while the composition is still open. Answering per section produces "none" every time, and adding either after a static page is specified produces decoration.

**Interaction.** Which single moment is worth participating in — or why this page has none? The plan's reader demands are the best evidence: a demand whose verb the reader performs is a candidate; a page of recognition and explanation honestly has none. "None, because every relationship here is better shown than operated" is a complete answer.

Where the answer names a moment, state for it: what the visitor learns by doing that they could not learn by looking, what the composition shows before any input, and what survives without script and under reduced motion. Essential meaning held behind a state the visitor may never open has hidden the page's argument.

**Meaning-bearing motion.** Where does movement itself explain something — a state changing, a sequence advancing, a cause producing an effect, responsibility moving from one place to another, a comparison resolving? Name the moment and what the movement carries. Foundational site motion is inherited and is not this decision; atmospheric motion is optional and subordinate.

Both are decisions, not quotas. What is not acceptable is never asking.

## Develop sections proportionally

After the composition is approved or supplied, develop sections to the depth each actually needs. Do not give every section the same treatment.

**A focal or complex experience** needs enough for someone else to build it reliably: job, payload, the relationship it makes visible, first-glance read, the moves that give it presence, its opening, evidence required, interaction and its states, desktop composition and dominant object, narrow-width transformation, and the thing most likely to fail in the render.

**A supporting section** needs its payload, the relationship it carries, its weight, and its narrow-width transformation.

**A quiet passage** needs one line: what it is for and how it is treated. Say so explicitly — an unfinished section and a deliberately quiet one look identical in a specification and different in a render.

Two things prevent a correct payload from still producing a flat section:

- **Say how it is built, not only what it means.** Moves worth having available — a payload with its own ground and different values inside it · a contrasting object rather than a changed band · the middle of the type scale · one dominant element · a surface change from its neighbour · a non-standard opening · something breaking its container. These are optional possibilities selected when the material creates the need, not a checklist, a quota or a variety programme. No section is expected to use several, and a solution outside the list is equally valid. A quiet section says *nothing, deliberately* — that is a valid answer, and leaving it blank is not.
- **Concentrate, don't spread.** A few moments carry the strong moves. Every section shouting is noise; no section doing anything is timid.

The test for whether a specification line is worth writing: would a different answer change what gets built? If not, do not write it.

## Options must differ in substance

Each option must differ in **relationship, material or spatial thesis** — not in container. Three arrangements of one content shape are one option presented three times.

Two checks: if two options could swap their copy without either breaking, they are one option. If two options name the same payload and occupy space the same way, they are one option.

## Check it against its neighbours

Judge every section against what sits above and below it, never on its own. A section strong in isolation and wrong beside its neighbours has failed.

Name the neighbours, and say what changes at each boundary — surface, density, reading effort, width or scale. A contact sheet shows this in one look; a section crop cannot show it at all.

## Redesign and audit-to-rebuild

Where an existing page is audited before a new one is designed, the audit is **evidence about what failed**, not material to preserve.

Carry forward only approved meaning, proof, claims and material the plan or the owner explicitly fixes. **Do not automatically preserve** the old page's section count, hero, surface sequence, component anatomy, reading paths or spatial envelope. Develop the new spatial thesis before treating any of the old structure as reusable, and derive the reader demands from the plan rather than reverse-engineering them from the old sections — otherwise the old page's structure silently becomes the new one's.

"Different" means a different argument, spatial thesis, reading path, dominant material or hero idea. A rearrangement of the same content shape is the same page.

Rebuilding one section *inside a page that is staying* is a narrower case: keep its job, its place in the argument, anything the plan pins, and its surface by default — a surface change moves its neighbours too, so it is a page decision. Everything else is free, and is where the new design comes from. Write down what the material could *become* before choosing any arrangement.

## A reference page, only if the user names one

`README.md` owns this rule. Applied here: use a reference only when the user names one, and do not go looking otherwise. Capture it and read the capture first; open source only if implementation understanding is needed.

```
pnpm capture:route -- --desktop --sections <route> <output-dir-outside-repo>
```

Study the standard of resolution reached and how weight is distributed. Do not shop its constructions. Reuse one only when this material independently creates the same need.

When redesigning an existing page, capture that page regardless, and read the full page before the section crops.

## Protect meaning and truth

Identify what the supplied plan marks fixed, adaptable, open and unavailable, and preserve it. Never invent proof, metrics, interfaces, client results, operational detail, pricing or professional capability to complete a design — a payload needing evidence the business does not have is a request, not a design decision, and belongs in what the page still needs.

Plan rows are meaning material, not object counts.

## Work both scales

Establish the composition, develop the important or uncertain moments, then let their real needs revise it. Continue until neither scale is merely accommodating decisions frozen at the other.

Do not design isolated strong sections and assemble them. Do not produce a page silhouette containing undecided sections.

## Stop and ask only when

- the page or scope cannot be identified;
- fixed material contradicts an authority;
- required proof or media is unavailable and the design depends on it;
- the user has retained a decision whose alternatives produce materially different work.

## Hand off

The composition is complete when the page has a stated spatial thesis, its environments and width behaviour, a hero derived from this page, a named dominant moment or a reason there is none, an eye path, a weight distribution, proof placement, a reading-path count, and an answer on interaction and meaning-bearing motion.

Section development is complete when each section has the depth its weight requires and no more.

Report for review. Build begins after that. Pass approved work to `mindwp-page-build`.
