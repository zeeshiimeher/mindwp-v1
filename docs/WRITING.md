# MindWP writing

This document owns how MindWP sounds in public, and **where each fact belongs**.

It does not own business facts ([STRATEGY.md](./STRATEGY.md)), permissions and claims limits ([FOUNDATION.md](./FOUNDATION.md)), page structure, section anatomy, visual design or information architecture.

## Who the reader is

Every page on MindWP's site is read by a business owner deciding whether to buy.

It is never read by a patient or by a client's customer.

So MindWP's own copy does not describe a patient's medical situation, explain how a practitioner treats someone, or speak in the voice of a clinic talking to its patients. When a page describes what happens to an enquiry, describe it as the owner sees it: a request arrives, it is acknowledged, it reaches a named person.

MindWP does write patient-facing copy **on client sites**. That is different work and does not change the register here.

## Voice

Calm, specific and commercially serious. Direct without being aggressive. Warm without being vague. Confident without unsupported claims.

- Short and medium sentences. One clear idea at a time.
- Simple words where they work.
- Use "we" and "you" naturally.
- Start from something the reader recognises, then say what MindWP does about it.
- Prefer real things: a call, a form, a message, a booking request, a review, a follow-up, a person who owns it, an agreed next step.
- Avoid: solutions, ecosystems, optimisation, growth machine, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, all-in-one. Use them only when they are the literal fact.

## Where a fact belongs

This is the most important rule in this document. Internal strategy must not become public explanation.

| Kind of fact                                         | Where it goes                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What the buyer gets, and why they would want it      | Main copy                                                           |
| Proof and visible work                               | Main copy and work pages                                            |
| A limit that stops a wrong expectation before buying | One short line beside the claim, or an FAQ                          |
| Detailed scope boundaries                            | Proposal                                                            |
| How something is built, connected or configured      | Meeting or proposal                                                 |
| How delivery, onboarding and management actually run | Meeting or proposal                                                 |
| What ongoing management covers                       | Say it is available; agree what it covers in a meeting              |
| Platform, account, subscription and data ownership   | Proposal and contract, and legal pages where disclosure requires it |
| Whether an existing website is kept or rebuilt       | FAQ or conversation, briefly                                        |
| The detailed definition of a useful enquiry          | Proposal                                                            |
| How services relate to each other internally         | Strategy only. Not public                                           |
| Why MindWP is organised this way                     | Rationale only. Not public                                          |
| Anything that varies per client                      | Sales conversation                                                  |

### The three questions

For any true fact, in order:

1. Does the buyer need it to **want** the work? → main copy.
2. Does the buyer need it **before buying**, to trust the work or avoid a wrong expectation? → one line, or an FAQ.
3. Does it only matter **once scope is being agreed**? → proposal or meeting.

If none apply, it is internal. Leave it out.

One more check: **does this fact exist mainly to protect MindWP?** If so, it belongs in the proposal or contract, not in marketing.

## How much mechanism to explain

Go **one level below the promise, never two**.

- Promise: enquiries reach a named person and get followed up.
- One level down, allowed: acknowledged, routed, owned, followed up.
- Two levels down, not public: form submission, webhook, pipeline stage, notification rule.

Explain mechanism only where it makes the responsibility believable. A reader who does not yet want the outcome will not read the method.

## What not to explain in public

- **Ordinary website anatomy, and how the work gets done.** Pages, sections, headings, forms and content blocks are not benefits, and neither is the build process, the handling process or how a form behaves. Name them only where a buyer needs them to understand scope.
- **The whole business model on every page.** The complete picture belongs in at most one place. Every other page assumes it and gets on with its own job.
- **Internal structures.** The enquiry path stages, the service family tree and the capability list are internal. They do not become section sequences or navigation.
- **Too many boundaries.** Boundaries should not dominate the main argument. Put a practical limit in a short line or an FAQ when a buyer needs it before purchasing. Keep detailed scope boundaries for proposals and meetings. A page that mostly says what MindWP will not promise has given nobody a reason to buy.

These apply to every page on the site, not only the homepage.

## Stay concrete, never vague

These rules cut internal explanation, not substance. Being short is not permission to be empty.

- Every service page must plainly say what MindWP actually does and what the client gets.
- Detail about **the buyer's world** is welcome. Detail about **MindWP's internal model** is not.
- Sending a fact to a proposal does not excuse a page that cannot say what is being sold.

**Test:** if a reader finishes a page unable to say what they would receive, the routing rules have been applied wrongly.

## Make it wanted

Truthful copy that gives no reason to buy has done half the job.

Lead with what becomes easier, clearer or more consistent. Then say what MindWP takes responsibility for. Bring in proof where the reader starts asking whether this is real. Carry the passage to a useful next step.

Good writing usually moves through six things, in whatever order the material wants:

- the situation the reader recognises — what is not working now;
- the improvement the buyer wants;
- what MindWP is responsible for;
- how it works, briefly;
- the proof;
- the limit.

Open with enough tension for the reader to recognise their own situation, then move on. Do not hold them in the problem, and do not manufacture a crisis.

These are dimensions, not a formula, a section order or a required set for every passage.

**Test:** every public passage should answer "why would this buyer want this?" A passage that only answers "what is technically true" is internal, however well written.

## Claims and proof

Foundation owns what may never be claimed. This document owns how permitted material is written.

- Write only what the evidence and the agreed scope support.
- Do not describe constructed or demonstration work in language that implies a real client, client brief or measured client result. How its status is shown is a page and design decision.
- Describe what can be inspected. Do not turn a good process into a measured outcome.
- Never use recycled industry statistics.
- Supporting growth is a claim about a mechanism and is allowed. Promising growth is not — including as an implication or a rounded-up paraphrase.

## Service language

Use the current names in Strategy: Smart Website Systems, Connected Enquiry Systems, Lead Response & Handling, Follow-Up & CRM, Reputation & Reviews, Local SEO, Visibility & Enquiry Review.

Planning shorthand does not authorise a public rename.

**Never shorten "Visibility & Enquiry Review" to "Review".** Written alone it collides with Reputation & Reviews, and a buyer reading about reviews will misread the action. Use the full name, or a plain action that does not use the word.

Phrasing risks to avoid:

- Local SEO must not read as domination, ranking promises or a guarantee.
- Lead Response & Handling must not read as an AI receptionist product.
- Follow-Up & CRM must not read as automatic pressure.
- Reputation & Reviews must not read as rating management.
- Smart Website Systems must not read as an ordinary website, or as a bundle that includes everything.

Explain CRM work through ownership, status, permission and a justified next action — not software features. Explain AI through the real task it supports, never as a replacement for judgement. Explain WordPress through maintainability and implementation quality, never as tool preference.

Advertising may be named as a source of attention. MindWP's role starts where that attention lands.

## FAQs and calls to action

FAQs answer real objections, scope questions and practical questions plainly, in a few lines each. They are the right home for facts that matter but do not deserve a section.

CTA copy explains what the action involves, who it is for and what happens next, at the level currently known. Keep it low-pressure and specific. Do not invent a price, a duration, a response time or a deliverable that Strategy has not settled.

## Repetition

Give each passage its own job: recognition, distinction, explanation, evidence, fit, objection or action.

Repeating the same benefit or mechanism under new headings weakens authority. Repetition is useful only when it confirms a decision at the right moment.
